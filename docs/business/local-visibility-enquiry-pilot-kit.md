# Local Visibility & Enquiry Pilot — First Commercial Pack

**Status:** ready for founder review and buyer discovery. This is a draft offer and operating template, not evidence of customer demand or legal approval.
**Target:** one narrow local service segment at a time. Start with a non-regulated category; regulated categories require additional qualification and policy checks.

## 1. Offer

### Working name
**Local Discovery & Enquiry Check**

### Outcome statement
A clear, evidence-led review of how easily potential customers can find, trust and contact a local business online, followed by a short list of prioritized improvements. No ranking, lead-volume or revenue guarantees.

### Ideal first customer
A small local service business with:
- a real, operating business and a reachable decision-maker;
- a website or business profile that can be reviewed with permission;
- a specific discovery/contact problem they recognize;
- capacity to act on recommendations;
- no need to access sensitive customer records for the initial audit.

Choose one category and one geography after buyer conversations; do not target everyone.

### Fixed scope (pilot hypothesis)
1. Intake call (up to 30 minutes) and explicit permission for any account/site access.
2. Baseline review of the website, public business profile, key service/location information and contact path.
3. Review of up to five prioritized issues across clarity, consistency, accessibility, mobile usability and enquiry friction.
4. A short action list split into quick fixes, owner decisions and out-of-scope work.
5. One agreed low-risk fix or guided handoff, if credentials/access and scope permit.
6. Before/after evidence and a concise findings call.
7. A recommendation to continue, adjust or stop; no automatic retainer pitch.

### Exclusions
- Guaranteed search ranking, lead volume, sales or revenue.
- Paid ads, full website rebuild, legal advice, reputation manipulation, fake reviews or purchased links.
- Unapproved edits to third-party profiles, hosting, DNS or production systems.
- Scraping gated data, collecting sensitive customer records, or bulk outreach.
- Regulated qualification claims unless verified against the applicable requirements.
- Ongoing support outside the written scope.

### Price hypothesis
Test a fixed price in the previously proposed £250–£750 range only after estimating delivery hours and asking buyers about value and alternatives. This is not a validated market rate. Offer a smaller discovery-only option if the pilot scope or buyer budget does not support the full package. State VAT/tax treatment correctly and agree payment terms before work.

## 2. Buyer discovery script (15–25 minutes)

Opening: “I’m researching how local [segment] businesses help people find the right service and make an enquiry. I’m not assuming you need a service; I’d like to understand your current process.”

1. How do most new customers find you today?
2. What kinds of enquiries are best for you? Which are poor fits?
3. Where do people get confused or drop off before contacting you?
4. How do you check that your website and public business information are accurate?
5. How often does that information need updating, and who does it?
6. What have you tried to improve visibility or enquiries in the last 12 months?
7. What did it cost in money and time? What worked or did not work?
8. What happens when a suitable enquiry is missed or arrives through the wrong channel?
9. How do you currently measure whether these efforts help?
10. If you could fix one issue this month, what would it be and why?
11. Who would approve a paid project, and what evidence would they need?
12. Would a fixed-scope review be useful? What would make it worth paying for? What would make it a no?
13. May I follow up with a one-page scope? If yes, by what channel and when?

Avoid leading questions and don't count politeness as intent. Record verbatim pain language only with permission; otherwise keep concise anonymized notes. Ask for no passwords, customer lists or confidential records.

## 5. Delivery and QA checklist

### Before
- [ ] Confirm scope, price, dates, cancellation/refund terms and customer approvals in writing.
- [ ] Confirm ownership/authorization for every account or system accessed.
- [ ] Record baseline with date, source and limitations.
- [ ] Agree success measure and what evidence can be collected.
- [ ] Use named accounts and least privilege; never ask for secrets in chat or documents.

### During
- [ ] Separate observed facts, customer statements and hypotheses.
- [ ] Prioritize by customer impact, confidence, effort and risk.
- [ ] Get approval before production edits or third-party changes.
- [ ] Keep a change log and rollback path.
- [ ] Do not create false listings, reviews, testimonials, links or credentials.

### After
- [ ] Capture comparable before/after evidence.
- [ ] Explain what changed, what did not, and what remains uncertain.
- [ ] Reconcile hours, API/hosting costs, refunds/credits and cash collected.
- [ ] Ask whether the problem recurs and whether ongoing help would be useful—without pressure.
- [ ] Request permission before using any name, quote, screenshot or case study publicly.
- [ ] Decide continue / adjust / stop based on evidence.

## 8. UK marketing/privacy and trust gate

Before any outbound campaign, check current ICO guidance and obtain suitable advice for the exact recipient type, channel and data use. Public contact information does not automatically permit marketing. Distinguish corporate subscribers from sole traders and ordinary partnerships under PECR; UK GDPR can also apply to personal data. Identify the sender, provide a valid opt-out where required, honour objections, and keep a suppression process. This checklist is operational guidance, not legal advice.

Paid placement in Real World Atlas must be visibly disclosed and must never silently change organic discovery ranking. Any future enquiry partnership needs clear user expectations, attribution, written terms, a lead-quality definition and a valid basis for any personal-data processing.

## 9. First decision gate

Do not build software from this pack alone. First:
1. choose one segment;
2. gather 20 dated pain signals from at least three sources;
3. conduct 5–10 buyer conversations;
4. send a scoped offer only when there is a fit;
5. assess willingness to pay, delivery cost, customer outcome and repeat demand.
If no one pays, record the result and revise or stop rather than describing the offer as validated.

# Real World Atlas AI Factory: 30-Day Rollout

## Mission
Build a repeatable AI-assisted software and growth operation that improves Real World Atlas / Life Lived Network while testing multiple revenue streams. Optimize for verified customer value, cash flow, quality, and reusable capability—not agent count or activity.

## Outcomes by day 30
- A documented, reviewed operating model and task contract.
- A baseline for repository health, product usage, SEO and revenue experiments.
- One bounded product journey improved end-to-end, with tests and independent review.
- One service offer tested with real prospects; report paid conversion honestly, including a zero-sale result.
- One SEO/content experiment with sources, human review and measurable outcomes.
- A ranked tool registry with license, privacy, cost and rollback checks.
- A weekly decision on what to continue, change or stop.

## Days 1–3: Establish control
1. Compare `life-lived-network`, `life-lived-network2`, and relevant companion repositories. Record purpose, default branch, activity, deploy target and source of truth. Do not merge or duplicate code until ownership is clear.
2. Read each active repository's `AGENTS.md`, invariants, architecture decisions, product constitution and CI workflow before changing code.
3. Baseline build, lint, tests, accessibility, errors, analytics, key product actions and current revenue.
4. Review secret handling and repository history. Do not copy secrets into issues, prompts or docs. Rotate credentials if exposure is confirmed.
5. Adopt the task contract, risk gates, PR-only workflow and a shared experiment log.

## Days 4–7: Validate the toolbox
1. Evaluate one SEO/marketing skill set and one critique/review skill.
2. Record upstream URL, license, exact files installed, dependencies, permissions, data sent externally, model/tool costs and removal steps.
3. Test on a non-production sample. Compare output quality, factuality, editing time and cost against a manual baseline.
4. Do not install entire repositories blindly or execute unreviewed scripts.

## Week 2: Deliver one product-and-growth loop
1. Choose one high-value user journey from discovery to a meaningful real-world action.
2. Audit its UX, mobile/accessibility, empty/error states, analytics and search discoverability.
3. Make one bounded improvement behind the existing architecture; add tests and have an independent reviewer inspect the diff.
4. Publish only reviewed, genuinely useful content. Track impressions, clicks, qualified actions and corrections.
5. Conduct customer discovery for the service offer; do not treat compliments as validated demand.

## Week 3: Test revenue
1. Offer a fixed-scope local-business visibility and enquiry improvement pilot.
2. Suggested hypothesis to test—not a market-rate claim: £250–£750 for a clearly bounded pilot, adjusted after prospect interviews and delivery-cost estimates.
3. Contact a small, relevant, manually reviewed prospect list with honest, individualized messages. Respect privacy, consent, opt-outs and applicable marketing law; no mass unsolicited automation.
4. Track replies, qualified calls, proposals, paid conversions, hours, direct costs, rework and gross margin.
5. Explore verified listing partnerships only where the platform offers real user value. No fabricated listings, reviews, availability or verification badges.

## Week 4: Standardize what worked
1. Review evidence, revenue, margin, product actions, SEO quality, agent acceptance rate, rework and incidents.
2. Automate only a proven repetitive step with bounded retries, spend limits, logs and a human escalation path.
3. Extract a shared primitive only after at least two real workflows demonstrate reuse.
4. Decide which experiments to scale, revise or stop. Keep unsuccessful results in the log.

## Revenue portfolio
- **Near-term service:** local SEO/content audit, conversion improvements and enquiry workflow setup.
- **Platform partnerships:** enhanced verified listings, referrals or booking-related revenue only after demand, data rights and unit economics are validated.
- **Digital products:** templates, local content kits and workflow packs; pre-sell or interview buyers before substantial production.
- **Automation implementation:** scoped workflow builds for a defined customer segment, with support boundaries.
- **Audio/guide services:** optional, permissioned narration/transcription/translation where delivery economics work.
- **Future recurring product:** subscription tooling only after repeated use and willingness to pay are demonstrated.

## Decision rules
- Prioritize paid evidence and user outcomes over vanity metrics.
- Do not run paid acquisition until conversion tracking and unit economics are credible.
- Do not promise rankings, leads, revenue or autonomous reliability.
- Stop experiments that lack a clear owner, measurable outcome, budget or review date.

# Revenue Experiments

## Purpose
Validate customer demand and delivery economics before scaling. These are hypotheses, not forecasts or guaranteed income streams.

## Initial experiments

### E1 — Local visibility and enquiry pilot
**Buyer:** one defined local service category (for example, trades or independent hospitality).
**Offer:** baseline audit, prioritized technical/on-page recommendations, a small set of genuinely useful page/content improvements, enquiry-path review and a measurement report.
**Price:** test a transparent fixed-scope quote. £250–£750 may be used as an initial hypothesis, not represented as an established market rate.
**Success:** a customer pays, the scope can be delivered profitably, and the buyer can understand the outcome. A no-sale result is useful evidence when outreach and follow-up were executed as planned.
**Guardrails:** no ranking guarantees, fake reviews, misleading claims, scraped personal data, bulk spam or unapproved changes to customer sites/accounts.

### E2 — Verified listing / partner demand
**Buyer/partner:** experience provider, venue, local organization or booking partner.
**Test:** interviews and a simple opt-in pilot for a verified profile or qualified referral.
**Success:** documented willingness to participate and a clear user benefit; measure profile accuracy, qualified referrals and retention.
**Guardrails:** source and update date for factual claims; explicit permission for submitted assets; correction/removal path; no paid placement presented as organic ranking.

### E3 — Reusable digital asset
**Buyer:** a narrow audience that has a recurring task.
**Test:** interview potential buyers or pre-sell a small template/guide/workflow kit before polishing.
**Success:** paid pre-orders or strong repeated evidence of a costly problem.
**Guardrails:** disclose limitations, licensing and included support; do not repackage third-party material without rights.

### E4 — Workflow automation setup
**Buyer:** a business with a repetitive, measurable workflow.
**Test:** map the existing workflow, quantify baseline time/error rate, then implement a limited pilot with human review and rollback.
**Success:** demonstrable time saved or error reduction that exceeds setup/support costs.
**Guardrails:** minimum access, approved data processing, logs, budget caps and human escalation.

## Unit economics
Track collected revenue, refunds, delivery hours, model/API cost, software fees, contractor cost, support/rework and acquisition cost. Report gross margin and owner time separately. Do not scale on revenue alone.

## Review cadence
Review weekly. Scale only when there is repeatable demand, acceptable margin, reliable delivery and manageable support/risk. Stop or redesign when the evidence misses the threshold after the agreed test window.

# Overnight R&D: ethical growth streams, SEO services, and Guildhall ADHD-centre pathway

**Research date:** 2026-10-09  
**Status:** Strategy/research only. No clinical, legal, or regulatory advice. No marketing campaign launched.  
**Owner decisions required:** canonical product repository; backend A/B/C; whether to authorize any external outreach, ad spend, clinical service changes, or production security changes.

## Executive recommendation

Run two commercial workstreams in parallel, with different risk controls:

1. **Cash-flow experiment: productized local SEO and enquiry-improvement service.** Sell a tightly scoped audit/fix/measurement package to a small number of suitable businesses. Deliver manually first, measure hours and outcomes, and automate only repeated work. Avoid mass outreach, fake local pages, guaranteed ranking claims, or paid placements disguised as organic results.
2. **Clinical growth workstream: The Guildhall Practices GP and medical treatments / holistic ADHD care.** Treat this as a regulated healthcare service-development programme, not simply another SEO vertical. Begin with patient access, service pathway, capacity, clinician governance, accurate information, accessibility, and compliant enquiry handling. Do not publish treatment promises or scale assessment/prescribing demand ahead of qualified staffing, operational capacity, and regulatory readiness.

Real World Atlas / Life Lived Network remains the long-term platform and should not be turned into an undisclosed lead-generation funnel. Keep the product's organic discovery and trust rules independent of any commercial placement or client relationship.

## Productized SEO service: initial offer hypothesis

### Offer A — Local Visibility & Enquiry Baseline
Deliverables:
- technical crawl and indexability check
- Search Console / analytics review only where the client grants access
- Google Business Profile completeness and policy review
- core local landing-page and service-page audit
- mobile accessibility and enquiry-path review
- structured prioritized fix list with impact/confidence/effort
- baseline measurement plan and 30-day check-in

Do not promise rankings, calls, leads or revenue. Quote only after scope and access are known. A prior £250–£750 pilot range is an unvalidated pricing hypothesis; test willingness to pay and delivery economics rather than treating it as market fact.

### Offer B — Implementation sprint
A bounded number of agreed fixes from Offer A, each with before/after evidence, QA, and rollback where applicable. Exclude complex migrations, backlink schemes, legal/compliance certification, and anything that cannot be completed within the quoted scope.

### Offer C — Monthly care
Monthly technical checks, agreed content/profile updates, reporting, conversion-friction fixes, and a short action review. Avoid vanity reports without decisions. Define cancellation terms and scope.

### Delivery unit economics
Track for every engagement:
- qualified opportunity source and consent/legal basis
- time spent by activity
- tool/API costs
- contractor costs
- client-approved access
- defects/rework
- delivered artifacts
- baseline and post-change indicators
- client outcome and renewal intent
- gross margin (actual, not forecast)

A service becomes a candidate for automation only after at least 3 comparable deliveries reveal a repeatable process.

## Ethical SEO growth system

1. Pick one customer segment and one geography; do not target every niche at once.
2. Build a list of 20 publicly evidenced issues from at least three source types, documenting URLs and dates. Treat findings as research, not permission to market.
3. Use client-authorized data (Search Console, GBP, analytics, CRM) only with least privilege.
4. Build a reusable audit rubric: crawlability, indexation, canonicalization, internal links, page intent, local business data, accessibility, performance, content usefulness, conversion clarity.
5. Create human-reviewed pages only when each page serves a real user need and contains unique local/service value. Do not mass-generate near-duplicate location pages or doorway pages.
6. Use Google Business Profile only in accordance with current guidelines; no fake addresses, review gating, incentivized reviews, or fabricated business attributes.
7. Distinguish technical findings, observed data, hypotheses, and recommended work in every report.
8. Get client approval before changing production websites, listings, DNS, tracking, ad accounts, or structured data.
9. Establish a repeatable before/after protocol and publish only permissioned case studies with verifiable evidence.
10. Use outbound marketing only after a method-specific compliance check. ICO guidance distinguishes corporate subscribers from sole traders/ordinary partnerships; publicly available contact details do not automatically mean consent. Keep a suppression list and honor objections.

## Guildhall Practices: holistic ADHD and medical-service growth

This is a separate, higher-governance subprogramme. First confirm the actual legal entity, service locations, regulator, registered activities, professional roles, current service scope, capacity, and whether the planned work is in England, Wales, or both. Do not assume ownership alone establishes regulatory authority for every service.

### Workstream A — Service and capacity map
- Catalogue currently offered GP and medical treatments from owner-approved materials.
- Map patient journey: discover → understand service → eligibility / suitability enquiry → admin triage → clinician assessment → care plan → follow-up / review.
- Identify where administrative delays, unclear eligibility, inaccessible information, missed follow-up, or repeat data entry cause friction.
- Separate admin triage from clinical triage; only qualified staff make clinical decisions.
- Model capacity by clinician hours, appointment duration, admin support, follow-up obligations, and safe wait-list size.
- Do not increase marketing demand faster than the practice can safely serve it.

### Workstream B — ADHD pathway readiness
Create a clinician-led service blueprint covering:
- service scope and target population
- qualified diagnostic and prescribing responsibilities
- assessment standards and developmental/psychiatric history requirements
- comorbidities and risk escalation
- medication baseline checks, monitoring and follow-up responsibilities where medication is offered
- non-pharmacological support and signposting
- shared-care arrangements only where explicitly agreed and operationally valid
- referral acceptance/exclusion criteria, wait-time transparency and complaints route
- reasonable adjustments, neurodiversity-affirming communication, sensory/access needs and accessible documents
- safeguarding, crisis escalation and continuity of care
- record keeping, confidentiality, data protection, retention, subject access and breach response
- clinician capacity, supervision, clinical audit, incident reporting and quality improvement

NICE NG87 is a core clinical guideline reference, not a substitute for professional judgement or local regulatory advice. Marketing must accurately describe what the service does and must not imply that a website quiz or AI tool diagnoses ADHD.

### Workstream C — Ethical patient acquisition
- Clear service pages: who the service is for, what is included, who provides it, qualifications, locations, fees, realistic process and waiting-time information where known.
- Explain assessment and treatment as clinical processes; avoid guarantees, “instant diagnosis”, unsupported outcome claims, fear-based copy, or claims that medication is suitable for everyone.
- Separate education from individualized medical advice.
- Use accessible, plain-language content reviewed by a suitably qualified clinician.
- Establish a clear administrative enquiry path that requests the minimum necessary information.
- Do not send sensitive patient information to marketing analytics, ad platforms, general-purpose AI agents, or SEO tools.
- Avoid patient-level retargeting, sensitive-condition audiences, or ad targeting that exposes a person's health status.
- Before paid search/social campaigns, review the current platform healthcare/medicine rules, destination requirements, geographic restrictions, and any certification requirements. A campaign may be restricted even when the underlying service is lawful.
- Do not purchase patient lists or use patient enquiries for unrelated marketing without a valid, separately documented basis and appropriate consent where required.

### Workstream D — Operations and technology
Candidate low-risk automation opportunities, subject to clinical/privacy approval:
- public FAQ and service information maintenance with clinician sign-off
- admin-only appointment request routing
- staff task reminders and follow-up queues
- document/version control
- anonymized capacity and wait-time reporting
- audit checklist and policy review reminders
- accessibility and website QA
- aggregated website funnel metrics that do not reveal health status

Do not automate diagnosis, prescribing, medication recommendations, risk assessment, patient eligibility decisions, or clinical prioritization without a formally governed clinical safety case and qualified oversight. Do not use real patient records in an agent sandbox. Use synthetic data for development.

## Agent team and overnight operating model

Agents should operate in bounded lanes with a shared issue queue and evidence log, not all editing the same files simultaneously.

| Lane | Objective | Output | Authority |
|---|---|---|---|
| Programme conductor | Prioritize dependencies, reconcile findings, morning brief | Decision log, top 3 queue | Can plan; cannot override owner gates |
| Product engineering | Small code changes and regression tests | Draft diff/PR | No production deploy or merge |
| Security/Supabase | Catalog/config evidence, threat model, proposed fixes | Findings and rollback plan | Read-only until explicit approval |
| SEO research | Official policy, SERP/user intent, audit rubric | Sources and offer hypothesis | No outreach or client changes |
| Revenue analyst | Offer economics, buyer hypotheses, unit-economics model | Experiment design | No invented revenue forecasts |
| Guildhall clinical operations | Service blueprint, capacity, regulatory question list | Clinician-review pack | No clinical claims or patient-data access |
| QA/release | Test plan, CI evidence, accessibility checklist | Pass/fail report | No weakening tests |
| Knowledge curator | Summarize decisions, link evidence, remove duplication | Updated docs/issues | No rewriting owner decisions |

Rules:
- One active code-writing agent per repository/branch at a time.
- Parallel agents may research independently, but publish findings into separate docs/issues before consolidation.
- No agent gets production credentials or patient data by default.
- No agent sends messages to prospects/patients, launches ads, changes client websites, changes Google Business Profiles, purchases tools, changes database grants, migrates schema, or deploys without explicit owner approval.
- Use a task contract: objective, authorized scope, prohibited actions, budget/time cap, acceptance criteria, test requirements, output format, escalation condition.
- Require each agent to report verified facts, assumptions, links, work done, tests run, failures, blockers, and next action.
- Stop duplicate work by checking the issue queue and active agent tasks first.

## 12-hour trial: measurable outputs, not artificial activity

Use a fixed timebox with checkpoints rather than asking agents to run indefinitely:
- Hour 0–1: establish owner gates, active task inventory, repository/branch status, no duplicate writes.
- Hours 1–3: official-source research and SEO service offer draft; Guildhall service/capacity questions.
- Hours 3–5: inspect SEO tool candidates in isolated/sandbox mode; do not install or connect production data.
- Hours 5–7: product tests/QA on authorized branch; stop if repository identity is unresolved for any write.
- Hours 7–9: model delivery economics and measurement plan; no fabricated projections.
- Hours 9–10: independent review of evidence and risks.
- Hours 10–11: consolidate docs/issues and list all approvals required.
- Hour 12: morning report with links, changed files, commits, tests, costs, blockers and next three tasks.

If no persistent runtime or scheduled execution is available, agents cannot be represented as continuously working. Report actual completed work and use a scheduled morning check-in rather than claiming 24/7 execution.
